# Strengthened-Edition Source Discipline

This edition uses a strict source hierarchy for definitions and legal statements:

1. **Kenyan legislation and EPRA material control legal/licensing definitions and scope.**
2. **Kenya Standards control Kenya-specific technical compliance where applicable.**
3. The uploaded BS 7671 book is used for electrical-installation terminology where it genuinely defines the term.
4. A consultation draft is identified as a draft and is not silently presented as enacted law.
5. A technical explanation is not labelled an “official definition” unless the source actually defines the term.

For long answers, use:

\[
\boxed{\text{Rule/definition}\rightarrow\text{what it requires}\rightarrow\text{why it matters}\rightarrow\text{practical consequence}}
\]

For a regulatory scenario, always identify:

\[
\boxed{\text{person}\rightarrow\text{premises}\rightarrow\text{scope}\rightarrow\text{authorization}\rightarrow\text{verification/documentation}}
\]


# Current Kenya Regulatory Position — Important Study Note

For this study bank, the current regulatory position is treated as follows:

1. **The Energy Act, 2019** is the principal statute governing electrical installation work in Kenya.
2. EPRA currently lists the **Electric Power (Electrical Installation Work) Rules, 2006** among its electricity regulatory instruments.
3. EPRA has published newer **draft** Energy (Electricity Supply and Installation Work) Regulations for consultation. They are useful for understanding future direction, but this study bank does **not** treat a consultation draft as enacted law.
4. EPRA's current published competency sheet for written and oral interviews is used to define the C2 and C1 examination scope.
5. Kenya Standards in the **KS 662** series form part of the national technical framework for electrical installations.
6. BS 7671 remains useful as a technical learning/reference source because the Kenya wiring standards draw heavily on IEC/BS principles, but BS 7671 is not to be quoted automatically as though it were itself Kenyan legislation.

# Section A — Regulatory Authorities and Legal Framework

## Question 1 — What Is EPRA?
**What is EPRA and what is its role in the electricity subsector? (5 marks)**

### Answer

EPRA is the:

\[
\boxed{\text{Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority}}
\]

It is the national regulator responsible for economic and technical regulation of the electricity subsector in Kenya.

Its functions include matters such as:

- licensing electrical workers and contractors;
- enforcing electricity regulations, codes and standards;
- monitoring compliance;
- setting and enforcing safety and quality requirements;
- investigating complaints and disputes within its mandate;
- sanctioning breaches of applicable energy law.

For an electrical worker, EPRA is especially important because it regulates who is authorized to carry out electrical installation work.

## Question 3 — Why National Regulations Matter
**Why is technical skill alone not enough for an electrical worker? (5 marks)**

### Answer

An electrician may understand:

- cable sizing;
- protective devices;
- testing;
- motors;
- earthing;

but still act unlawfully if the work is carried out outside the worker's authorization or without the required contractor/licensing arrangements.

A competent electrical worker therefore needs both:

\[
\boxed{\text{technical competence + regulatory compliance}}
\]

This includes knowing:

- what work the licence permits;
- who may undertake the work;
- what certificates are required;
- what inspection/testing is required;
- which national standards apply.

## Question 4 — Act, Regulations and Standards
**Differentiate between an Act of Parliament, regulations/rules and a technical standard. (6 marks)**

### Answer

A distinction answer should identify the **hierarchy and authority** of each source.

### Act of Parliament

An Act is primary legislation passed through the national legislative process.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Energy Act, 2019}}
\]

It establishes broad legal duties, powers, offences and regulatory authority.

### Regulations or Rules

Regulations/rules are subsidiary legislation made under powers granted by an Act.

They provide more detailed operational requirements.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{Electric Power (Electrical Installation Work) Rules, 2006}}
\]

### Technical Standard

A standard provides technical requirements, specifications or methods.

Example:

\[
\boxed{\text{KS 662 series}}
\]

A standard is not the same thing as an Act, though legislation, contracts or regulatory requirements may require compliance with it.

# Section B — Authorization to Carry Out Electrical Installation Work

## Question 5 — Energy Act Section 148
**What is the main requirement of section 148 of the Energy Act, 2019 regarding electrical installation work? (4 marks)**

### Answer

Section 148 requires a person who wishes to carry out electrical installation work to do so through the licensing framework administered by the Authority.

A person carrying out installation work as an electrical contractor must be licensed by EPRA.

To qualify as an electrical contractor, the person must either:

- be a certified electrical worker;
- or employ a certified electrical worker.

The main principle is:

\[
\boxed{\text{electrical installation work is a regulated activity}}
\]

---

## Question 6 — Certified Worker vs Licensed Contractor
**Differentiate between a certified electrical worker and a licensed electrical contractor. (6 marks)**

### Answer

### Electrical Worker

An electrical worker is the technically certified person authorized to carry out the class of electrical work specified in the worker's certificate.

### Electrical Contractor

An electrical contractor is the person/business licensed to undertake electrical installation work as a contractor.

Under the Energy Act, a contractor must:

- be a certified electrical worker;
- or employ a certified electrical worker.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{worker certification and contractor licensing are related but not identical}}
\]

A worker certificate demonstrates the individual's authorized technical class.

A contractor licence authorizes the contracting business activity.

---

## Question 7 — Why the Distinction Matters
**Why is it incorrect to assume that holding an electrical-worker certificate automatically gives unlimited authority to undertake any electrical contracting work? (5 marks)**

### Answer

There are two separate issues:

1. **technical class/scope** of the worker;
2. **contractor authorization** for undertaking electrical installation work as a business/contractor.

The work must also remain within the class of work for which the person is authorized.

Therefore:

- holding a worker certificate does not remove contractor-licensing requirements;
- holding a licence does not allow work beyond the technical class/scope.

---

## Question 8 — Working Outside Licence Scope
A worker is competent in practice but undertakes installation work outside the class authorized by the worker's certificate.

**Why is this unacceptable? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Licensing limits the class or scope of work the worker is authorized to carry out.

Practical confidence does not legally expand the licence.

Working outside the authorized scope can:

- breach regulatory requirements;
- invalidate certification;
- expose consumers to unsafe work;
- lead to enforcement action.

The correct approach is:

\[
\boxed{\text{work only within the class and conditions of authorization}}
\]

**Why does EPRA use written and oral competency assessment? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Electrical work creates direct risks of:

- electric shock;
- fire;
- equipment damage;
- unsafe installations.

A qualification certificate alone may not prove that the applicant understands:

- practical installation work;

- testing;

# Section F — Duties of the Owner or Occupier

## Question 20 — Section 151 Owner/Occupier Duty
**What duty does section 151 of the Energy Act place on the owner or occupier of premises regarding electrical installation work? (6 marks)**

### Answer

The owner or occupier must ensure that the electrical installation is:

- carried out only by a duly authorized electrical contractor;
- appropriately certified before initial connection;
- periodically inspected and tested;
- repaired where defects are identified;
- supported by the appropriate installation documentation/certification.

The owner therefore has a legal responsibility and cannot lawfully ignore who carries out the installation.

---

## Question 21 — Owner Hires Unlicensed Person
A homeowner knowingly hires an unauthorized person to carry out electrical installation work because the price is cheaper.

**Why is the homeowner also at fault? (5 marks)**

### Answer

The Energy Act does not place responsibility only on the person physically carrying out the work.

The owner/occupier has a duty to ensure that installation work is carried out by an authorized electrical contractor.

Therefore knowingly permitting unauthorized installation work can itself constitute an offence.

---

## Question 22 — Periodic Inspection
**Why does the Energy Act require electrical installations to be periodically inspected and defects remedied? (5 marks)**

### Answer

An installation can deteriorate after initial commissioning due to:

- ageing;
- loose connections;
- moisture;
- physical damage;
- unauthorized alterations;
- overloading;
- environmental effects.

Initial safety does not guarantee permanent safety.

Periodic inspection helps identify deterioration before it causes:

- electric shock;
- fire;
- equipment damage.

# Section G — Connection to Electricity Supply

## Question 23 — Certification Before Initial Connection
**Why must installation certification be completed before initial connection to the electricity supply? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Connection energizes the installation.

Before that happens, there should be evidence that the work has been:

- completed;
- inspected;
- tested;
- verified safe.

Certification provides formal confirmation that the installation has undergone the required verification.

Connecting unverified wiring can expose occupants and utility personnel to immediate danger.

---

## Question 24 — Utility Refuses Connection
An electrical installation fails safety inspection.

**Can the connection provider be required to energize it simply because the owner has paid for connection? Explain. (5 marks)**

### Answer

No.

Under the Energy Act, supply may be refused where the electrical installation does not satisfy the required conditions.

Safety takes precedence over the owner's desire for immediate connection.

Defects must be corrected before the premises is accepted for safe supply.

---

## Question 25 — Discontinuation of Existing Supply
**Can an existing electrical supply be discontinued where an installation is found to be unsafe? Explain. (4 marks)**

### Answer

Yes.

The Energy Act provides for supply to be declined or discontinued where installation conditions required by the Act are not met.

This protects:

- occupants;
- property;
- utility personnel;
- the distribution system.

Supply can be restored after the defects are properly remedied and the applicable requirements are satisfied.

# Step 8 Distinction Lens — A Certificate Is a Technical Declaration

Certification should be understood as the final stage of:

\[
\boxed{\text{design}\rightarrow\text{erection}\rightarrow\text{inspection}\rightarrow\text{testing}\rightarrow\text{assessment}\rightarrow\text{declaration}}
\]

A certificate is not made valid by a signature alone.

The person signing must have a defensible basis for the declaration, supported by:

- inspection;
- measured test results;
- correct scope;
- accurate identification of the work;
- professional responsibility.

This is why signing unverified work is fundamentally unsafe.

# Section I — Unauthorized Electrical Work

## Question 30 — Section 152
**What is the general purpose of section 152 of the Energy Act, 2019? (4 marks)**

### Answer

Section 152 creates offences and penalties relating to unauthorized electrical installation work.

It addresses:

- unauthorized persons carrying out electrical installation work;
- consumers permitting unauthorized work;
- entities permitting unauthorized persons to undertake electrical work.

The purpose is to discourage unsafe and unregulated installation activity.

---

## Question 31 — Unauthorized Worker Penalty
Under section 152, what penalty may apply to a person carrying out electrical installation work without being duly authorized? **(4 marks)**

### Answer

The Act provides for a penalty on conviction of up to:

\[
\boxed{KSh\,100,000}
\]

or imprisonment for up to:

\[
\boxed{6\text{ months}}
\]

or both.

The important principle is that unauthorized installation work is an offence, not merely poor professional practice.

---

## Question 32 — Consumer Allowing Unauthorized Work
What penalty may apply to a consumer who permits unauthorized electrical installation work in the premises? **(4 marks)**

The Act provides for a penalty on conviction of up to:

\[
\boxed{KSh\,50,000}
\]

or imprisonment for up to:

\[
\boxed{3\text{ months}}
\]

or both.

This reinforces the owner's legal duty to use duly authorized persons.

---

## Question 33 — Why Penalties Apply to Consumers Too
**Why does the law penalize a consumer who knowingly allows unauthorized electrical work? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Unsafe installation work affects more than the person doing the work.

It may endanger:

- occupants;
- neighbours;
- utility staff;
- firefighters;
- future electricians;
- property.

If only the unlicensed worker were responsible, consumers might deliberately hire cheaper unauthorized installers.

The law therefore places responsibility on both sides of the transaction.

# Section K — Draft Regulations: Exam Caution

## Question 38 — Draft vs Enacted Regulation
**Why must a student distinguish between a draft regulation and an enacted regulation? (5 marks)**

### Answer

A draft is a proposal published for:

- consultation;
- comment;
- review;
- possible amendment.

It may never come into force in exactly the published form.

An enacted regulation has completed the required legal process and has legal effect from the applicable commencement date.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{draft text must not automatically be quoted as current law}}
\]

---

## Question 39 — Current EPRA Draft Position
**What is the safe exam approach to the newer Energy (Electricity Supply and Installation Work) draft regulations? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Use them only as:

- proposed/future regulatory direction;
- background information.

For a current-law answer, rely on:

- the Energy Act, 2019;
- regulations/rules currently listed as operative by EPRA;
- current official EPRA competency and licensing information;
- applicable Kenya Standards.

If an examiner specifically asks about a draft proposal, identify it clearly as a draft.

## Question 40 — Why Draft Licence Classes Can Mislead
**Why is it dangerous to memorize licence-class names from consultation drafts instead of the current EPRA competency document? (5 marks)**

### Answer

Draft regulations have proposed revised class structures at different stages.

Those proposed structures do not necessarily match the current C2/C1 examination classes.

Therefore using draft class labels could cause the student to:

- reverse C1/C2 scopes;
- quote future proposals as current law;
- give the wrong exam answer.

For this study bank, the current official EPRA competency sheet is used for C2/C1 assessment scope.

# Step 8 Distinction Lens — Law and Standards Are Different

Do not use the words **law**, **regulation** and **standard** as though they mean the same thing.

- An Act creates legal duties and powers.
- Subsidiary regulations/rules give more detailed legal requirements.
- A technical standard gives technical requirements/methods and may become mandatory through law, licence conditions, contracts or regulatory incorporation.

The correct exam answer identifies which type of source is being discussed.
