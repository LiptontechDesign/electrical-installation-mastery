## What the student actually needs to do

The recurring numerical chain is `Ns=120f/P`, `s=(Ns−Nr)/Ns`, `Nr=(1−s)Ns`, and `fr=s f`. For a balanced three-phase motor, `Pin=Pshaft/η` and `Iline=Pin/(√3 Vline PF)`. For correction at fixed input active power, `Qc=Pin[tan(acos PF1)−tan(acos PF2)]`; single-phase `C=Qc/(2πfV²)`, with Qc in var. Bank connection changes the voltage/current seen by each capacitor.

Independent arithmetic checks against the motor givens:

| Paper case | Checked result / condition |
|---|---|
| November 2012: 7.46 kW shaft, η=0.85, 400 V three phase, PF=0.8 | **15.83 A** line current. Do not omit efficiency. |
| November 2012 / October 2016: 1000 W electrical input, 220 V, 50 Hz, PF 0.7→0.9 | **535.88 var**, then **35.24 µF** in parallel. This is not a motor-start capacitor calculation. |
| March 2013: 10 kW shaft, η=0.84, 400 V single phase, 50 Hz, PF 0.7→0.9 | **126.92 µF**, using **11.905 kW input**. The single-phase 400 V value is the problem's given supply. |
| May 2022 / May 2025: four poles, 50 Hz, 5% full-load slip; question specifies 0% no-load slip | **1500 rpm synchronous/no-load by assumption**, **1425 rpm full load**; at 60 Hz and the same stipulated slip, **1710 rpm full load**. A supplied 1800 rpm answer at 50 Hz is inconsistent. |
| September 2023: eight poles, 50 Hz, 5% full-load slip; 60 Hz no-load variant | **750 rpm synchronous**, **712.5 rpm full load**; **900 rpm** at 60 Hz only under the ideal zero-slip no-load assumption. |
| May 2022 motor/AHU and May 2025 motor correction | 100 kW input at PF 0.8→0.97 needs **49.94 kVAr**. The 415 V, 120 A, PF 0.7→0.95 AHU needs **41.75 kVAr**. Source solution arithmetic is not copied. |

For star–delta, compare the **same motor, line voltage and starting impedance**: star winding voltage and winding current are **1/√3** of direct-delta values; **supply line starting current and ideal torque are about 1/3**. Those fractions do not compare a starting current directly with rated full-load current. The motor must be suitable for running in delta at that supply. These distinctions and the slip-ring starting principle agree with ABB's [Softstarter Handbook, printed pp. 6–9 and 13–15](https://library.e.abb.com/public/6b4e1a3530814df0c12579bb0030e58b/1SFC132060M0201.pdf). This historical technical source is not asserted to be a current Kenyan code document.
