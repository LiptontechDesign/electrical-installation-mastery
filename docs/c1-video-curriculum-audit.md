# C1 video pathway audit — 2026-09-23

Source of scope: [EPRA, Assessment Areas for Electrical Worker Written & Oral Interviews (June 2025)](https://www.epra.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-06/Written%20%26%20Oral%20Interviews%20Areas%20of%20Competency.pdf). Its C1 row explicitly includes the C2 competencies and adds low-voltage three-phase systems in buildings up to four storeys (excluding factories and public-entertainment venues), three-phase calculations, cable/protection sizing, special protective devices, small-commercial earthing, power-factor correction and device sizing, three-phase motors and controls, and initial/periodic inspection and testing. Do not substitute older or draft licence-class descriptions for this published competency list.

For the local standards bridge, the [Kenya National Building Code 2024, section 312](https://www.kenyalaw.org/kl/fileadmin/pdfdownloads/LegalNotices/2024/LN47_2024.pdf) cites **KS 662 (Electrical Installations)**, alongside other Kenyan standards. UK BS 7671 examples are useful instruction, but the current Kenyan requirements control real work. A 2026 KEBS draft must not be treated as an adopted edition without verification.

## Why this order works for a self-taught video learner

1. **Three-phase supply, star/delta and neutral behaviour:** see the system before using formulas. The six existing core videos progress from overview and physical demonstration to star/delta and balanced/unbalanced loads.
2. **Voltage, current and power calculations:** consolidate line/phase quantities, then calculate balanced three-phase power, power factor and line current. State when the balanced formula is valid.
3. **Boards, feeders and outlets:** safe isolation precedes practical board footage; identify how a board and submain fit together before design or wiring examples.
4. **Load, cable and protection design:** estimate demand, size cable/protection and check three-phase voltage drop before practical installation footage. The smart-board tour remains available as optional deep practice.
5. **Small-commercial earthing and installation:** use the C2 earthing foundation; study the design fault-loop lesson before its Part 2 temperature correction. Then apply the completed design and fault path to board-installation examples. The small cable-tie lesson remains optional depth.
6. **Power-factor correction:** move from causes/consequences through a worked kVAr bank calculation to the choice of individual, group, central or automatic placement.
7. **Motors and terminals:** field, rotor and slip precede nameplate interpretation and star/delta terminal arrangement.
8. **Starters, protection and speed control:** contactor and DOL first, then motor-protection sizing, wiring and a phase-failure relay; finally compare star-delta, soft starter and VFD. Short-circuit protection, overload sensing, switching and speed control are distinct functions.
9. **Initial and periodic verification:** reuse C2 inspection/dead-test foundations; learn phase-sequence checking immediately before three-phase live verification, then periodic condition reporting.
10. **Fault diagnosis:** understand lost neutral and trace conductors before board-fault cases; add motor single-phasing and finish with thermal evidence, which is optional depth rather than a substitute for electrical tests.

No canonical video ID, URL or completion record is replaced. Supplementary videos are placed beside a lesson without creating an associated quiz or mock exam.

| Low-voltage three-phase installation, up to four storeys | Supply/neutral theory; three-phase board, submain, plugs/outlets; demand/design; two installation case studies. | A complete Kenyan small-commercial project walkthrough from drawings and load schedule to inspection/handover is still absent. | Three-phase voltage, current and power | Star/delta line/phase lessons, existing calculation lesson, and three worked supplementary videos. | Balanced `sqrt(3) × V_LL × I_L` shortcuts do not describe every unbalanced/non-sinusoidal board; compare phases and account for motor efficiency when output kW is given. |
