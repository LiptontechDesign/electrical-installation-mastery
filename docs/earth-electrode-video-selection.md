Pipe tutorials that prescribe salt/charcoal, claim suitability for every soil/weather condition, or treat rods and pipes as physically identical were excluded. Water/gas service pipework must not be presented as a substitute for an intentionally designed electrode. The plate and DEHN videos give international examples; their dimensions and jurisdiction-specific rules are not Kenyan installation specifications.

## The supplied Schneider page

Read [Installation and measurements of earth electrodes](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Installation_and_measurements_of_earth_electrodes). Its ring, rod/pipe and plate discussions informed the selection, together with soil, seasonal changes and corrosion considerations.

The page describes two measurement arrangements:

- **Pairwise ammeter method:** measure the three electrode-pair resistances A, B and C; the electrode under test has resistance **(A + C − B) / 2**. This is different from taking several fall-of-potential readings. No sufficiently strong reviewed video demonstrated this exact pairwise method; retain the page as the reference. Kanumuri explains the voltage/current principle and AC testing, not this exact three-pair procedure.
- **Direct-reading earth tester:** current and potential stakes plus repeated readings to establish a stable result. John Ward and Megger demonstrate this.

The page uses an approximate 0.68 probe position, while the videos discuss 62%. These are not interchangeable universal rules. The appropriate geometry, sufficient separation and a validated stable curve matter; follow the tester's instructions and the applicable test procedure.

## Additional primary references

- [IET: TT earthing considerations](https://electrical.theiet.org/wiring-matters/years/2021/87-september-2021/tt-earthing-considerations/) — UK electrode choices and TT context.
- [Fluke: fall-of-potential](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/grounding/earth-ground-fall-potential) — auxiliary electrodes and valid measurement geometry.
- [Fluke: stakeless method](https://www.fluke.com/en/learn/blog/grounding/stakeless-method) — needs a suitable parallel earth return; a lone isolated rod cannot be measured this way. A metallic loop can produce a misleading low reading.
- [Megger: soil resistivity testing](https://www.megger.com/en-us/blog/august-2014/let-s-get-technical-soil-resistivity-testing) — Wenner survey principles.
- [GreyMatters: field tips and transcript](https://greymattersglobal.com/soil-resistivity-helpful-tips/) — original UK specialist explanation.

Soil resistivity (ohm-metres), electrode resistance (ohms) and installation loop impedance (ohms) answer different questions. UK examples such as 200 ohms, 21 ohms or RCD-based calculations must not be turned into a universal Kenyan acceptance limit. Kenyan requirements, approved designs and equipment instructions remain the basis for real installations, as stated in the existing course safety notice.
