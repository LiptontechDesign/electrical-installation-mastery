# Electrical Installation Mastery — Assessment Source Map

## Purpose

This document is the source and design blueprint for adding meaningful retrieval practice to the 16-module course. It is not a bank of copied book questions. Every final flashcard, question, explanation and scenario must be newly written, traceable to a learning objective, and adapted to Kenyan residential and light-commercial practice.

The intended learning loop is:

1. Watch the lesson.
2. Review a concise concept summary.
3. Complete a short lesson recap.
4. Continue to the next video or choose another lesson.
5. Complete a substantial mastery review at the end of the module.
6. Revisit missed or low-confidence material through spaced repetition.

## Source hierarchy and regulatory guardrails

Use sources in this order when statements conflict:

1. Current Kenyan legislation, EPRA requirements, Kenya Building Code, applicable Kenya Standards, Kenya Power requirements and the approved project specification.
2. Applicable KS/IEC product and installation standards.
3. BS 7671 and IET guidance where Kenya expressly accepts them or where they provide clearly labelled comparative technical guidance.
4. Manufacturer instructions for the actual equipment.
5. Textbooks and videos as teaching explanations, not as legal authority.

The Energy Act requires electrical installation work to be carried out by appropriately authorised people and requires installation, certification and periodic inspection duties to be observed. The National Building Code 2024 identifies KS 662, KS 1587, a series of KS IEC standards and BS 7671 among the permitted electrical-installation references.

### C1/C2 terminology

The operative Electric Power (Electrical Installation Work) Rules, 2006 define:

- **C2:** single-phase low-voltage work in residential and commercial buildings of up to two storeys, excluding factories and places of public entertainment.
- **C1:** C2 work plus three-phase low-voltage work in buildings of up to four storeys, with the same exclusions.

A proposed **Energy (Electricity Supply and Installation Work) Regulations, 2024** text would transition C1 to class C and C2 to class D, with scope expressed by supply voltage and specialised-installation exclusions. The source published in December 2024 is a draft for public comment, so course questions must not present that transition as current law until formal commencement is verified.

Specialised work—such as generators, EV charging, hazardous areas, lifts, power lines and some renewable-energy activities—must be taught as awareness, coordination and licence-bound practice where applicable. Completing this course is not a licence or authorisation to perform such work.

## Assessment model

### After every video

Each lesson should contain a **2–4 minute recap**:

- 3–5 flashcards covering the minimum durable knowledge.
- 2–4 questions: at least one understanding question and one application question.
- One short explanation for every answer, including why plausible wrong choices fail.
- A confidence choice: `Unsure`, `Almost`, or `Confident`.
- A `Review before next` setting. When enabled, auto-next starts only after the recap is completed or explicitly skipped.

Very short or introductory videos may use two cards and two questions when extra items would be artificial. Complex design, testing, lighting, smart-system or fault-finding lessons may use six cards and five questions.

### At the end of every module

Each module should contain:

- A 20–30 card flashcard deck.
- A 15–30 item mastery quiz, sized to the module.
- At least two realistic Kenyan scenarios.
- At least one safety or stop-work decision.
- A practical planning task, diagram, measurement interpretation or calculation where the subject allows it.
- A pass threshold of 80%, with unlimited learning attempts.
- A review deck automatically populated from wrong answers, low-confidence answers and lapsed cards.

### Question mix

Use a balanced mix rather than relying on recognition-only multiple choice:

- **Recall:** name, purpose, symbol, terminology.
- **Explain:** why a protective measure, wiring method or sequence is necessary.
- **Sequence:** arrange isolation, testing, first-fix or commissioning stages.
- **Select:** choose equipment or a method from environmental and load information.
- **Diagnose:** move from symptom to the safest discriminating test.
- **Interpret:** read a drawing, schedule, test result, label or product data.
- **Calculate:** units, power, current, diversity, conductor checks or voltage drop.
- **Client scenario:** turn activities and preferences into a specification.
- **Kenyan site scenario:** masonry, stone or brick walls; reinforced slabs; gypsum ceilings; heat, dust, rain, voltage variation, water storage, pumps and backup supplies.
- **Scope and compliance:** recognise when current rules, a specialist licence, manufacturer evidence or competent supervision are required.

## Module-by-module source and assessment map

### Module 1 — Electrical Foundations

**Competencies:** electrical quantities and units; series/parallel behaviour; AC basics; magnetism; transformers and machines; hazards; safe isolation; basic tools and measurement.

**Primary sources:** Basic Level 2 chapters 1–2; Advanced Level 3 chapters 1 and 3; Schneider sections A and S.

**Lesson checks:** identify hazards before action; explain voltage/current/resistance; distinguish overload, short circuit and earth fault; predict open- and short-circuit symptoms; select a measuring function and connection method; recognise when not to test live.

**Module mastery:** 25 questions, 25 cards and a paper/extra-low-voltage circuit-tracing task. Include a Kenyan domestic supply scenario and a safe-isolation stop-work scenario.

### Module 2 — Understanding Building Electrical Systems

**Competencies:** supply-to-load path; service position; earthing arrangements; distribution boards; circuits and accessories; drawings; maximum demand; room requirements.

**Primary sources:** Installation Designs chapters 1–3 and 9; Modern Wiring chapters 3–5; IET On-Site Guide sections 1–2 and 7; Schneider sections A, C–E and Q.

**Lesson checks:** trace the energy path; distinguish service equipment from consumer equipment; read symbols and single-line diagrams; translate household activities into outlets and fixed loads; identify missing site/supply information.

**Module mastery:** develop a room schedule and simple single-line diagram for a Kenyan stone or brick home, then answer 20 questions about interfaces, assumptions and circuit grouping.

### Module 3 — Practical Wiring

**Competencies:** conductors and cables; terminations; clipping and support; conduit; trunking; bends and draw-in; concealed and surface systems; workmanship.

**Primary sources:** Basic Level 2 chapters 3–4; Modern Wiring chapters 8–15; Installation Designs chapter 14.

**Lesson checks:** choose a wiring method from external influences; order conduit/trunking work; diagnose poor terminations; plan bend and draw-in access; identify mechanical-protection and support failures.

**Module mastery:** 25 questions, 30 cards and a route-planning exercise comparing concealed conduit in chased masonry, slab conduit, surface conduit and trunking.

### Module 4 — Commercial & Industrial Installation

**Competencies:** three-phase concepts; distribution; industrial plugs and sockets; containment; switchgear; motors; phase balance; commercial load patterns.

**Primary sources:** Advanced Level 3 chapter 3; Installation Designs chapters 5, 6, 8 and 12; Modern Wiring chapters 5 and 10–12; Schneider sections D–H, L–N.

**Lesson checks:** interpret phase/line relationships; allocate single-phase loads; select containment for impact, heat or future change; identify motor-control functions; separate isolation, control and protection.

**Module mastery:** a small shop/workshop distribution scenario with phase-balancing, equipment selection and 25 mixed questions. Keep factory work clearly beyond C1/C2 scope where the operative licence rules apply.

### Module 5 — Protection & Earthing

**Competencies:** shock protection; automatic disconnection; overcurrent devices; RCDs/RCBOs; SPDs/AFDD awareness; earthing and bonding; fault paths; selectivity.

**Primary sources:** IET On-Site Guide sections 3–4 and 11; Installation Designs chapters 4, 7 and 17; Schneider sections F, H and J; Advanced Level 3 chapters 3 and 6.

**Lesson checks:** distinguish overload from fault protection; trace an earth-fault loop; determine what an RCD can and cannot do; identify bonding purpose; choose the next check after unwanted tripping; recognise outdated or jurisdiction-specific requirements.

**Module mastery:** 30 questions, 30 cards and three fault-path diagrams. Include correct-device-but-wrong-breaking-capacity, lost protective-conductor continuity and nuisance-RCD scenarios.

### Module 6 — Design & Calculations

**Competencies:** design inputs; load assessment; diversity; design current; protective-device and conductor coordination; correction factors; voltage drop; fault withstand; prospective fault current; documentation.

**Primary sources:** Advanced Level 3 chapter 6; Installation Designs chapters 1 and 19; Modern Wiring chapters 3–6; IET On-Site Guide appendices 1, 2, 4–6 and 8; Schneider sections A and G.

**Lesson checks:** identify missing assumptions; perform unit and power calculations; apply a fixed design sequence; find the first failed design gate; explain why current capacity alone does not prove suitability.

**Module mastery:** a 30-item calculation and judgement quiz plus a worked domestic circuit audit. Numerical questions must carry a data-source/version tag and show the method, not only the answer.

### Module 7 — Complete Building Distribution

**Competencies:** incoming supply; main switchgear; submains; distribution-board schedules; circuit zoning; essential loads; discrimination; phase balance; labels and diagrams.

**Primary sources:** Modern Wiring chapters 5–6; Schneider sections C–H; IET On-Site Guide sections 2, 5–7 and appendices 7, 10–11; Installation Designs project chapters.

**Lesson checks:** build a hierarchy from source to final circuit; separate landlord/tenant or normal/essential supplies; select useful labels; identify single points of failure; evaluate board capacity and future spares.

**Module mastery:** produce a distribution schedule and single-line concept for a multi-level residence or small commercial building within the learner’s licence scope, followed by 25 questions.

### Module 8 — Inspection, Testing & Commissioning

**Competencies:** initial verification; visual inspection; safe sequence of dead and live tests; instruments; expected results; certificates; commissioning and handover.

**Primary sources:** IET On-Site Guide sections 9–11 and appendix 7; Advanced Level 3 chapter 5; Modern Wiring chapter 16; Installation Designs chapter 15; Schneider section S.

**Lesson checks:** order tests and justify dependencies; select an instrument; recognise an invalid test condition; interpret results; decide whether energisation may proceed; identify incomplete records.

**Module mastery:** 30 questions, 30 cards and a commissioning-sequence exercise with hold points. Exact limits and form requirements must be tagged `regulation-sensitive` and verified before publication.

### Module 9 — Fault Finding

**Competencies:** symptom definition; safe information gathering; fault hypotheses; discriminating tests; continuity/insulation/polarity reasoning; intermittent faults; repair verification.

**Primary sources:** Advanced Level 3 chapter 4; Basic Level 2 science and installation chapters; IET On-Site Guide testing sections; Schneider sections F, G, H, J and S.

**Lesson checks:** choose the safest next test; predict readings; distinguish cause from symptom; avoid random component swapping; prove repair and document limitations.

**Module mastery:** five branching cases—dead circuit, repeated protective-device operation, lighting flicker, earth-leakage symptom and motor/pump failure—plus 20 questions.

### Module 10 — Complete Project Thinking

**Competencies:** brief; survey; assumptions; design; coordination; programme; installation; change control; verification; handover; post-occupancy learning.

**Primary sources:** Installation Designs all project chapters; Advanced Level 3 chapter 6; Modern Wiring chapters 3 and 6; Basic Level 2 communication chapter.

**Lesson checks:** identify the next project deliverable; allocate responsibility; record a variation; detect missing interfaces; explain how a late architectural change affects electrical work.

**Module mastery:** capstone portfolio review with 20 questions and an evidence checklist covering brief, drawings, calculations, method, inspection, tests, labels and client information.

### Module 11 — Residential First-Fix, Accessories and Client Specification

**Competencies:** setting out; masonry chasing; box depth and alignment; concealed conduit; slab coordination; kitchen/bathroom accessories; socket and isolator choices; as-built records.

**Primary sources:** Modern Wiring chapters 8–10, 13 and 15; Basic Level 2 chapters 3–4; Installation Designs chapters 2, 9 and 14; IET On-Site Guide section 7.

**Lesson checks:** choose chase/box/conduit sequence; coordinate with structure, plumbing and plaster; check box depth for smart controls; classify accessory purpose; locate accessible isolation; update an as-built drawing.

**Module mastery:** mark up a Kenyan masonry home before plastering. Include slab-to-wall conduit transitions, deep boxes, kitchen fixed loads, external outlets and inspection before covering.

### Module 12 — Lighting Specification, Fittings and Controls

**Competencies:** ambient/task/accent/decorative lighting; lumens; distribution and beam angle; CCT and colour rendering; glare; IP/IK; downlights; gypsum coordination; LED strips and drivers; dimming; sensors; DALI/smart control; emergency lighting.

**Primary sources:** Schneider section A load characteristics, sections K and Q; Modern Wiring chapter 15; Advanced Level 3 lighting content in chapter 3; Installation Designs chapters 5, 6, 12 and 18; IET On-Site Guide final-circuit and special-location sections.

**Lesson checks:** select a fitting from the visual task and environment; compare lumen output rather than wattage alone; locate accessible drivers; protect fire/acoustic boundaries; avoid insulation/thermal conflicts; diagnose flicker/dimming incompatibility; plan switching from user behaviour.

**Module mastery:** a room-by-room lighting schedule for a Kenyan masonry home with gypsum ceilings. Use 30 questions and 35 cards spanning bedrooms, kitchen, bathroom, stair, exterior, security, landscape and emergency lighting.

### Module 13 — Smart Buildings, Data, Security and Life Safety

**Competencies:** smart-switch/relay architecture; neutral and box-depth planning; manual overrides; scenes and sensors; structured cabling; fibre/Wi-Fi/PoE; segregation; CCTV; access control; gates; alarms; fire-detection interfaces.

**Primary sources:** Modern Wiring chapter 15 data circuits; Schneider sections E, K, N, Q and R; Advanced Level 3 devices/electronics content; Installation Designs office and project chapters.

**Lesson checks:** distinguish bus, wireless and relay arrangements; identify first-fix provisions; choose cable pathways and segregation; position access points/cameras from function; preserve manual operation; recognise life-safety and specialist boundaries.

**Module mastery:** coordinated services plan for a three-bedroom home and compound, with 25 questions on architecture, maintainability, power/backup, cybersecurity awareness and handover.

### Module 14 — Solar, Storage, Backup Power and EV Readiness

**Competencies:** site survey; PV array/inverter/battery architecture; DC and AC isolation; essential-load boards; source transfer; generators/UPS; earthing and SPDs; monitoring; load management; EV readiness.

**Primary sources:** Schneider sections J, N and P; IET On-Site Guide sections 2, 6 and 12; Modern Wiring special-installation PV content; Advanced Level 3 environmental technologies; Installation Designs isolation/protection/design chapters.

**Lesson checks:** trace each energy path and operating mode; identify backfeed and alternative-supply labels; separate essential from non-essential loads; check transfer interlocking; interpret battery/inverter ratings; recognise specialist licensing and manufacturer dependencies.

**Module mastery:** single-line and operating-mode review plus 30 questions. Solar PV, generators and EV charging must be presented as separately regulated/specialised work where current Kenyan requirements say so.

### Module 15 — Building Services, Motors, Ventilation and Surge Protection

**Competencies:** motor data; starters/contactors/overloads; pumps and level/dry-run controls; water heating; ventilation; HVAC interfaces; surge risk; maintenance access; commissioning.

**Primary sources:** Advanced Level 3 electrical machines and control content; Modern Wiring sections 5.7 and 12; Schneider sections H, J and N; Installation Designs workshop/shop/sports-centre chapters.

**Lesson checks:** separate overload from short-circuit protection; read a starter/control schematic; choose manual/automatic control; diagnose no-start and nuisance-trip symptoms; coordinate isolators and access; relate surge exposure to electronic loads.

**Module mastery:** pump and bathroom-ventilation scenario for a gypsum-ceiling home, supported by 25 questions and a commissioning checklist.

### Module 16 — Professional Tools, Estimating and Handover

**Competencies:** task-based tool selection; instrument care and proving; surveying; cable detection; thermal inspection awareness; quantity take-off; labour/overhead; quotation; variation control; records; labels; certificates; client demonstration.

**Primary sources:** Basic Level 2 communication and safety chapters; Advanced Level 3 chapters 1, 4–6; IET On-Site Guide sections 6 and 9–10 plus appendix 7; Installation Designs chapters 1 and 15; Modern Wiring chapters 1, 8 and 16.

**Lesson checks:** select tools from the task/risk; identify calibration or proving needs; distinguish estimate, quotation and variation; find exclusions; audit a handover pack; recognise claims that only an authorised person may make.

**Module mastery:** prepare and critique a house-rewire proposal and handover pack, then complete 25 questions on traceability, commercial clarity, testing evidence and client communication.

## Flashcard writing rules

Each card must test one durable idea. The front should be answerable without seeing options; the back should contain:

- A direct answer in one or two sentences.
- A brief `why it matters` explanation.
- A Kenyan application or caution when useful.
- A source tag at chapter/section level.
- `regulationSensitive: true` when a legal limit, test value, device requirement, certificate or scope rule could change.

Avoid cards that merely ask for a page number, duplicate a video title or test trivia. Convert lists into decisions or grouped mental models.

## Quiz writing rules

- Never copy textbook review questions verbatim.
- Do not infer the content of a video from its title alone; use its transcript or a verified content review.
- Every distractor must represent a plausible misconception.
- Explanations must address the learner’s decision, not merely repeat the correct option.
- A numerical answer must show units, method and assumptions.
- Safety-critical questions must explain the stop condition and required competence.
- Regulation-sensitive questions need a source name, edition/date and review date.
- Do not award completion credit merely for opening a card or question.

## Traceability data model

Each lesson assessment should carry:

```ts
type AssessmentSource = {
  sourceId: string;
  chapterOrSection: string;
  jurisdiction: 'Kenya' | 'KS/IEC' | 'UK-reference' | 'manufacturer' | 'video';
  editionOrDate?: string;
  regulationSensitive: boolean;
  reviewedAt: string;
};

type LessonAssessment = {
  lessonId: string;
  objectives: string[];
  flashcards: Flashcard[];
  questions: Question[];
  sources: AssessmentSource[];
  transcriptVerified: boolean;
};
```

Module mastery items should link to one or more lesson objectives, so the results can show exactly which lesson to revisit.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Add the assessment schema, source/version labels and review scheduling.
2. Pilot complete lesson and module assessments in Modules 1, 5, 8, 11 and 12; these cover foundations, protection, testing, Kenyan first-fix and lighting.
3. Verify every target video against a transcript or direct content review.
4. Perform a technical and Kenyan regulatory review of all safety- and regulation-sensitive items.
5. Expand to the remaining modules, using missed-question analytics to improve weak explanations and distractors.
6. Add the module mastery gate and spaced-review dashboard only after the pilot content is accurate.

## Completion standard

The assessment system is ready for publication only when every video has verified learning objectives and its short recap, every module has a balanced mastery review, all answers include explanations, all regulation-sensitive claims have dated sources, and mobile use has been checked for cards, diagrams, calculations and long question text.
