white robust also have a version of their strip called the ambient which has a colour temperature of 2,200 Kelvin which is a really warm white in fact you'll also see these terms being used to describe the light output from White LEDs things like warm white neutral white and cool white so which would you want to use well it really depends on the application if the space your lighting is used for resting and relaxing then a warm white light is better as it can create a cozy restful atmosphere if however the space is more practical like a kitchen being used to prepare a meal then something cooler can help to create a dynamic active Vibe while providing good colour rendering too but what if the space is used differently at different times again maybe the kitchen is a kitchen Diner and you want the lighting to change from a cool white while you're cooking to a warm white when the food is served and it's time to relax and enjoy the meal the answer then is to use a tuneable LED strip this type of strip allows you to change the colour temperature of the light output using either a simple remote control or even an app in some cases so we can start out with this strip running at 2700 Kelvin and keep adjusting it until it hits the maximum level of cool white at 6,500 Kelvin which is designed to mimic natural daylight anything beyond this and it's starting to resemble a neutron star which is probably a bit too intense for most people then we come to RGB strip this type has LEDs that each give off a single colour either red green or blue hence the initials when you combine different levels of each of these light output colours you can get a huge range of almost infinitely variable colours this type of strip is really great where you want to create a really powerful effect if your installation calls for just one of the colours to be used it is actually possible to only connect up one of the individual colour connections out of the driver this can then be controlled by a simple on off switch to produce the single colour effect saving on the cost of installation and improving the running costs the next thing to consider is where the strip is the contacts that can start to cause damage to the strip and the driver that's why this type type of strip is rated at ip20 meaning it offers no protection to the Ingress of moisture in many situations this is never going to be a problem if the strip is installed indoors in a place where it's not likely to get moisture on it then it'll be fine if the strip is installed somewhere that it might get a bit wet from time to time then it may be time to look at a strip that has a higher IP rating for example this LED strip from robust comes in both ip20 and IP67 ratings The Seven at the end
