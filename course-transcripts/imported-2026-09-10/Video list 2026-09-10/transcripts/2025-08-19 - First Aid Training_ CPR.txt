# First Aid Training: CPR
# https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsJ49Np3HS0

If an adult is unresponsive and not breathing normally, you need to perform CPR straight away. CPR stands for cardiopulmonary resuscitation. It combines chest compressions and rescue breaths to give a person the best chance of survival following a cardiac arrest. If you find someone collapsed, make sure it's safe to approach. Check if they respond by gently shaking their shoulders and asking them loudly, are they okay? If they don't respond, shout for help and open their airway by gently tilting the casualty’s head back and lifting their chin. Look, listen and feel for up to 10 seconds for normal breathing. If they are unresponsive and not breathing, you should call 999 or 112 straight away. Use the hands free speaker on a phone so you can start CPR while speaking to ambulance control. Start CPR. Kneel by the casualty and put the heel of your hand in the middle of their chest. Put your other hand on top of the first and interlock your fingers. Keep your arms straight and lean over the casualty so your shoulders are over your hands. Press down hard to a depth of about 5 to 6 centimetres before releasing the pressure, allowing the chest to come back up. Repeat the compressions 30 times at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute. The beat of the song ‘Staying Alive’ can help you keep the right rate. If someone is with you, ask them to find a defibrillator. You can use www.defibfinder.uk to locate your nearest one. Otherwise wait for the ambulance to bring one. After 30 compressions you need to give two rescue breaths. If possible, use a face shield to protect from infection. Tilt the head back with one hand on the forehead and lift the chin with two fingers from the other hand to open the airway. Pinch the soft part of the nose shut and let the mouth open. Keep the head tilted, take a breath, and seal your mouth over theirs. Blow for one second until the chest rises then remove your mouth and watch the chest fall. If the chest doesn't rise, make sure the airway is open. If you're not trained or don't want to give rescue breaths, keep doing chest compressions without stopping. Keep doing CPR with 30 chest compressions and two rescue breaths until - Emergency help arrives and takes over. The person starts breathing normally. You are too tired to continue. A defibrillator is ready to use. If someone is helping, switch every 1 to 2 minutes with as little pause as possible. If the helper returns with a defibrillator, ask them to switch it on and follow the voice prompts while you continue with CPR. Keep checking their response and be ready to start CPR again if needed. Each year, 30,000 people have an out of hospital cardiac arrest in the UK. Less than 1 in 10 survive. Giving CPR promptly and using a defibrillator can more than double someone's chance of survival. You should - check for breathing. Call emergency services. Give 30 chest compressions. Give two rescue breaths. Continue 30 to 2 until help arrives. If they respond, put them in the recovery position.
